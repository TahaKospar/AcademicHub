# PART 2 — PRODUCT OVERVIEW

## 2.1 What Is AcademicHub?

AcademicHub is a customizable digital learning and student-management platform designed initially for English language academies.

The platform gives students, instructors, and academy administrators a shared digital environment in which they can manage their learning experience without relying on multiple disconnected applications and communication channels.

Instead of requiring students to navigate between WhatsApp groups, separate websites, manually shared documents, external task links, and scattered instructor messages, AcademicHub organizes the essential learning activities in one application.

The platform connects four major areas:

1. Student learning management.
2. Educational materials and assignments.
3. Instructor feedback and student progress.
4. Communication and academy administration.

AcademicHub is not intended to be a single application permanently tied to one academy.

It should be designed as a reusable platform that can serve multiple academies, with each academy able to configure its identity, logo, colors, courses, groups, instructors, schedules, and educational content.

For example, one academy could use AcademicHub with its own name and blue branding, while another academy could use the same underlying platform with a different name, logo, and color palette.

The platform should keep each academy's information securely separated.

## 2.2 Product Vision

Create a professional learning environment that makes the student's educational journey clearer, more organized, and easier to follow while giving instructors and academy administrators better tools to manage the learning process.

## 2.3 Product Mission

Transform a fragmented academy learning experience into a centralized digital experience where students know:

* What they should study.
* What they need to complete today.
* When their next lecture takes place.
* Which materials they need.
* How they are progressing.
* What their instructor thinks about their work.
* Where they can communicate with their group.
* What they need to improve.

At the same time, instructors should be able to manage their students, review their work, provide feedback, organize group communication, and monitor progress.

Academy administrators should be able to manage the overall system through a dedicated web dashboard.

## 2.4 The Problem AcademicHub Solves

Many language academies combine several tools to deliver their educational experience.

A typical student might receive a lecture schedule through one channel, educational files through another, task links through messages, reminders through group conversations, and instructor feedback through private messages or voice notes.

This can create several problems:

* Students may forget their daily tasks.
* Important messages can disappear inside group conversations.
* Students may not know what they need to complete next.
* Educational materials become difficult to find.
* Instructors may struggle to track individual student progress.
* Feedback can become scattered across different conversations.
* Academy administrators may lack a unified overview of learning activities.
* The student experience may depend too heavily on manual communication.
* Different academies may need the same core system with different branding and content.

AcademicHub addresses these problems by organizing the experience around a clear set of interconnected features.

## 2.5 Product Positioning

AcademicHub should be presented as:

**A customizable digital learning and student engagement platform for language academies.**

It should not be positioned merely as:

* A simple educational app.
* A video meeting application.
* A chat application.
* A static calendar.
* A collection of educational links.
* A generic university management system.

The product's value comes from integrating these essential learning workflows into one coherent platform.

# PART 3 — TARGET USERS AND USER ROLES

AcademicHub must support three primary roles:

1. Student.
2. Instructor.
3. Academy Support / Administrator.

Each role has different responsibilities, permissions, and interface requirements.

A user must never gain access to functionality merely because a screen or button is visible. All permissions must also be enforced by the backend.

## 3.1 Student

The student is the primary mobile application user.

Students use AcademicHub to organize their learning activities and interact with their academy.

The student can:

* Sign in or create an account.
* Join the appropriate academy.
* Access assigned courses and groups.
* View their learning plan.
* Browse a calendar of educational tasks.
* View daily and upcoming tasks.
* Open assigned external practice links.
* Mark tasks as completed when permitted.
* Access course materials and files.
* Participate in group discussions.
* Communicate with instructors through supported channels.
* Receive instructor feedback.
* Listen to instructor voice feedback when available.
* Track their learning progress.
* Receive notifications and reminders.
* Update their profile and preferences.
* Change the application theme.
* Manage notification preferences.
* View relevant course and group information.

Students must only access their own private information and content available to their assigned courses and groups.

They must not be able to modify instructor evaluations or access another student's private records.

## 3.2 Instructor

The instructor is responsible for managing assigned groups and supporting student learning.

The instructor interface should prioritize practical teaching workflows instead of unnecessary administrative complexity.

Instructors can:

* Sign in to their instructor account.
* View assigned groups.
* View students belonging to their assigned groups.
* Access group schedules.
* View relevant educational tasks.
* Create and manage tasks when authorized.
* Attach educational resources when authorized.
* Review student task submissions when submissions are supported.
* Record task completion or review status.
* Provide written feedback.
* Record or upload voice feedback.
* Identify pronunciation issues through instructor observations.
* Track student progress.
* Communicate with students and groups.
* Review relevant student activity.
* View upcoming lectures and tasks.

Instructor permissions must be configurable.

For example, an instructor may be allowed to review tasks but not modify academy-wide branding, manage subscriptions, or create new administrator accounts.

Instructors must not automatically gain access to every student or every group in the platform.

## 3.3 Academy Support / Administrator

The academy support role primarily uses a web dashboard.

This role is responsible for organizing and managing the academy's digital environment.

The dashboard should allow authorized administrators to:

* Configure academy information.
* Upload the academy logo.
* Configure branding and theme colors.
* Manage students.
* Manage instructors.
* Create and manage courses.
* Create and manage student groups.
* Assign instructors to groups.
* Assign students to groups.
* Configure schedules.
* Manage tasks and educational materials.
* Manage academy announcements.
* Review high-level progress information.
* Manage access codes.
* Handle account-related support.
* Review relevant activity logs.
* Configure notification defaults.
* Manage permissions where authorized.

The administrator should not need to manually edit application source code every time an academy changes its logo, adds a course, or creates a student group.

Those settings should be managed through the dashboard and delivered dynamically to the mobile application.

## 3.4 Future Platform-Level Administration

The system architecture should allow a future platform owner role.

This role would manage the overall AcademicHub service rather than a single academy.

Potential responsibilities include:

* Creating academy tenants.
* Managing platform-wide configuration.
* Managing academy activation.
* Monitoring service health.
* Managing platform-level subscription plans.
* Handling platform-level administrative operations.

This is a future extensibility requirement, not necessarily part of the first MVP.

Do not confuse a platform administrator with an academy administrator.

# PART 4 — MULTI-ACADEMY ARCHITECTURE

## 4.1 Core Requirement

AcademicHub must be designed for multiple academies from the beginning.

Each academy represents a separate organizational tenant.

A tenant can have its own:

* Academy name.
* Logo.
* Branding colors.
* Contact information.
* Courses.
* Groups.
* Instructors.
* Students.
* Educational materials.
* Tasks.
* Schedules.
* Announcements.
* Communication spaces.
* Administrative settings.

## 4.2 Dynamic Branding

The mobile application should load the relevant academy configuration after the student joins or signs in.

The configuration may include:

* Academy display name.
* Logo URL.
* Primary color.
* Secondary color.
* Accent color.
* Course information.
* Academy contact details.
* Welcome message.
* Optional academy-specific interface settings.

The application should use a shared design system while applying academy-specific branding where appropriate.

Branding changes must not break contrast, accessibility, component consistency, or usability.

Administrators should not be able to inject arbitrary executable code or unrestricted custom HTML into the mobile application.

## 4.3 Academy Isolation

Every academy-specific record must be associated with the correct academy identifier.

Examples include:

* Students.
* Groups.
* Courses.
* Tasks.
* Materials.
* Messages.
* Feedback.
* Notifications.
* Schedules.

The backend must validate academy membership and resource ownership.

A student belonging to Academy A must not be able to access Academy B's private records by changing a document ID, API parameter, or client-side state.

The system must enforce tenant isolation through backend authorization and database security rules, not through UI filtering alone.

# PART 5 — AUTHENTICATION AND ACCOUNT ACCESS

## 5.1 Authentication Requirements

AcademicHub should support:

* Email and password registration.
* Email and password login.
* Password visibility toggle.
* Password reset.
* Sign-out.
* Persistent authentication sessions.
* Appropriate loading and error states.
* Account profile management.

Google and Microsoft sign-in should be supported as optional authentication methods when the relevant provider configuration and platform requirements are available.

Do not display a provider login button as a working feature until its authentication flow has been implemented and tested.

## 5.2 Registration Flow

The registration form should include:

* Full name.
* Email address.
* Password.
* Confirm password.
* Academy code or invitation code.
* Acceptance of applicable terms and privacy policy.

The academy code identifies the academy or invitation context.

The backend must validate that the code is legitimate and that the account is eligible to join the academy.

A user must not be able to join a private academy merely by entering an arbitrary academy identifier.

The registration process should create the account and establish the correct academy membership.

If the academy requires administrator approval, the system should support a pending-access state rather than granting unrestricted access immediately.

## 5.3 Login Flow

The login screen should support the intended account identifiers.

The product may allow login using:

* Email address.
* Student ID, when student-ID authentication has been implemented.

Student ID login must not be simulated by placing a student ID into an email field.

If the backend uses Firebase Authentication, implement student-ID login through a secure, server-controlled mapping or another explicitly designed authentication mechanism.

Never expose a privileged lookup mechanism that allows unauthorized users to enumerate accounts.

The login screen should include:

* Email or supported student identifier.
* Password.
* Show/hide password control.
* Remembered authentication session.
* Forgot password.
* Sign-in action.
* Optional Google sign-in.
* Optional Microsoft sign-in.
* Navigation to account creation.
* Clear validation messages.

## 5.4 Account Provisioning

The academy may already maintain a list of students and instructors.

The system should support importing existing academy records through the administrator dashboard.

A recommended workflow is:

1. The administrator creates or imports a student record.
2. The system generates or assigns a unique student identifier.
3. The student receives an invitation or access code.
4. The student creates an account or links an existing account.
5. The backend verifies the invitation.
6. The system establishes academy membership.
7. The student receives the correct group and course assignments.

If a school or academy wants a simplified access-ID login without passwords, treat that as a separate authentication design decision requiring appropriate safeguards.

Do not implement insecure authentication merely to reduce friction.

# PART 8 — HOME SCREEN: STUDENT DASHBOARD

## 8.1 Purpose

The Home screen gives students an immediate overview of their current learning situation.

When a student opens the application, they should quickly understand:

* What is happening today.
* What needs attention.
* What has been completed.
* What is coming next.
* Whether their instructor has provided important feedback.

The screen should not feel like an administrative analytics dashboard.

It should be a friendly, practical student workspace.

## 8.2 Header

The header should include:

* Academy logo.
* Personalized greeting.
* Student display name.
* Profile avatar or initials.
* Notification icon with unread indicator.

Example:

"Good evening, Ahmed"

Supporting text:

"Ready to make progress today?"

The name and greeting must be dynamic.

Do not hardcode example student names in production data.

## 8.3 Today's Overview

Show a compact summary of today's learning activity.

Possible indicators:

* Tasks assigned today.
* Tasks completed.
* Remaining tasks.
* Upcoming lecture.

For example:

"Today's Learning"

* 4 tasks.
* 2 completed.
* 2 remaining.

These values must be calculated from actual task data.

Do not display invented progress percentages or random statistics.

## 8.4 Progress Overview

Provide a clear summary of the student's progress.

Possible indicators:

* Weekly task completion.
* Course progress, when a course-completion model exists.
* Recent activity.
* Completed tasks.

Do not confuse task completion with language proficiency.

Completing 80% of assigned tasks does not mean that a student has achieved 80% English proficiency.

Use accurate labels such as:

* Weekly Task Completion.
* Learning Activity.
* Course Progress, only when a defined course-progress calculation exists.

## 8.5 Upcoming Activities

Display the next relevant events, such as:

* An upcoming lecture.
* A task deadline.
* A scheduled practice activity.
* An instructor review awaiting attention.

Each item should show:

* Activity title.
* Relevant date and time.
* Activity type.
* Current status.
* Appropriate navigation action.

## 8.6 Recent Feedback

Show a small preview of the latest instructor feedback.

For example:

* Feedback title.
* Instructor name.
* Date.
* Short text preview.
* Voice-feedback indicator, if available.
* Action to open the full feedback.

Do not duplicate the entire feedback-management experience on Home.

The Home screen provides a preview; the Feedback screen provides the complete review workflow.

## 8.7 Recent Announcements

Display important academy announcements when relevant.

Announcements may include:

* Schedule changes.
* Course updates.
* Group notices.
* Important deadlines.

Only authorized academy personnel should publish official announcements.

## 8.8 Empty State

If a new student has no tasks or activity, show an intentional empty state.

Example:

"Your learning journey starts here."

Supporting text:

"Your upcoming activities will appear here when your academy assigns them."

Do not fill the screen with fabricated tasks to make it look populated.

# PART 9 — PLAN SCREEN: CALENDAR AND TASK MANAGEMENT

## 9.1 Purpose

The Plan screen organizes the student's learning activities over time.

It is the primary destination for answering:

"What do I need to do today, and what comes next?"

The Plan section must remain focused on scheduling and tasks.

It is not the educational file library, the discussion room, or the instructor-feedback archive.

## 9.2 Calendar Interface

Provide a clear calendar interface with:

* Monthly overview.
* Weekly overview where appropriate.
* Selected-day view.
* Navigation between dates.
* Indicators for dates containing tasks.
* Indicators for upcoming lectures.
* Clear distinction between completed and pending activities.

The calendar must use actual assigned dates and the correct timezone.

Avoid overloading the calendar with too many colors or decorative indicators.

## 9.3 Task Categories

Support the educational activity types used by the academy.

Examples include:

* Listening.
* Reading.
* Speaking practice.
* Writing.
* Vocabulary.
* Grammar.
* Pronunciation.
* General practice.
* Instructor-assigned work.

The category list should be configurable so that the academy can adapt the platform to its curriculum.

These categories describe the nature of a task, not necessarily the complete course structure.

## 9.4 Task Model

Each task should support relevant fields such as:

* Task identifier.
* Academy identifier.
* Course identifier, if applicable.
* Group identifier, if applicable.
* Task title.
* Description.
* Category.
* Assigned date.
* Due date.
* Estimated duration.
* External practice URL, when applicable.
* Attached resource references, when applicable.
* Assigned student or group.
* Task status.
* Completion timestamp.
* Creator identifier.
* Creation timestamp.
* Last-updated timestamp.

Only fields relevant to the task type should be required.

For example, a task that links to an external listening exercise may need a URL, while an instructor-reviewed writing task may need a submission workflow.

## 9.5 Task Statuses

Use a consistent task-status model.

Suggested statuses:

* Upcoming.
* Pending.
* In Progress.
* Submitted, when submissions are supported.
* Completed.
* Overdue.
* Needs Revision, when instructor review is supported.

Do not use all statuses indiscriminately.

Define the permitted transitions for each task type.

For example, a simple external practice task may move from Pending to Completed.

A writing assignment may move from Pending to Submitted, then Reviewed or Needs Revision.

The application must distinguish between:

* Student-confirmed completion.
* Instructor-reviewed completion.
* Automatically detected activity.

These are not equivalent.

## 9.6 Task Details

When a student opens a task, show:

* Task title.
* Description.
* Category.
* Assigned date.
* Due date.
* Estimated duration.
* Instructions.
* External link or resource reference.
* Current status.
* Relevant submission controls.
* Completion action, when permitted.

If a task uses an external website, provide an appropriate action to open the link.

Validate URLs and handle missing or invalid links safely.

## 9.7 Completing a Task

When a student marks a task as complete:

1. Validate the action.
2. Update the task's student-specific completion record.
3. Store the completion timestamp.
4. Refresh relevant progress indicators.
5. Update the Home screen.
6. Update calendar indicators.
7. Recalculate the appropriate completion statistics.
8. Provide a concise success message.

If the task requires instructor verification, preserve the distinction between student completion and instructor approval.

A student must not be able to mark another student's task complete.

## 9.8 Task Assignment Rules

Tasks may be assigned to:

* An entire group.
* A specific student.
* A course cohort.
* Another explicitly authorized audience.

If a task is assigned to a group, the system should maintain individual completion records for each student.

Do not store a single shared completion flag on the group task and assume that every student has completed it.

This distinction is essential for accurate progress tracking.

## 9.9 Task Reminders

Students should be able to configure their preferred reminder time.

For example, a student might choose 5:00 PM for daily learning reminders.

The application should support:

* Reminder enable/disable.
* Preferred reminder time.
* Relevant reminder frequency.
* Appropriate handling of task deadlines.
* Respect for device permissions.
* Duplicate-notification prevention.

A reminder should be useful rather than intrusive.

Do not send notifications for every minor database update.

# PART 10 — MATERIALS SCREEN: EDUCATIONAL FILES AND RESOURCES

## 10.1 Purpose

The Materials screen provides an organized library of educational resources.

It answers:

"Where can I find the file, document, or learning resource I need?"

## 10.3 Organization

Resources may be organized by:

* Course.
* Group.
* Topic.
* Skill.
* Resource category.
* Publication date.

Examples:

* Listening Practice.
* Reading Materials.
* Grammar Notes.
* Vocabulary Lists.
* Speaking Resources.
* General Course Files.

## 10.4 Material Card

Each material card should display:

* Resource title.
* File type or resource category.
* Short description.
* Associated course or group.
* Publication date, when relevant.
* File size, when available.
* Relevant action.

Actions may include:

* Open.
* Preview.
* Download.
* Open external link.

## 10.5 Search and Filtering

# PART 11 — CHAT SCREEN: REAL-TIME GROUP DISCUSSION

## 11.1 Purpose

AcademicHub must include a real chat feature.

This must not be a decorative chat mockup or a screen containing only predefined sample messages.

The Chat screen is the communication destination for students and authorized instructors.

It should support meaningful group discussion related to the educational experience.

## 11.2 Chat Types

The initial implementation should prioritize:

1. Group chat.
2. Instructor-to-student communication, subject to the configured permissions.

Private student-to-student messaging should not be enabled automatically.

The academy should be able to control which communication types are allowed.

## 11.3 Chat List

The main Chat screen should display the conversations available to the current user.

Each conversation item may contain:

* Group or conversation name.
* Conversation avatar.
* Last-message preview.
* Last-message timestamp.
* Unread-message indicator.
* Relevant participant information.

Only conversations that the user is authorized to access should appear.

## 11.4 Conversation Screen

The conversation interface should support:

* Real-time message delivery.
* Text messages.
* Message timestamps.
* Sender identification.
* Read or delivery indicators where supported.
* Unread-message handling.
* Message history.
* Loading older messages.
* Appropriate empty states.
* Network error handling.
* Sending and receiving states.

The interface must remain usable when messages are long or when the keyboard is open.

## 11.5 Additional Message Types

The architecture may support:

* Images.
* File attachments.
* Voice messages.
* System announcements.

These features should be introduced according to the MVP priorities and security requirements.

Do not implement every possible message type in the first version if doing so would compromise the reliability of the core text-chat experience.

## 11.6 Permissions

The backend must verify:

* The sender belongs to the academy.
* The sender is a member of the conversation or group.
* The sender has permission to post.
* The recipient or group belongs to the same authorized context.
* The referenced attachments are accessible to the intended audience.

A client must not be able to impersonate another sender by changing a user ID in a message payload.

The backend should establish the authenticated sender identity.

## 11.7 Chat Notifications

Users should receive appropriate notifications for relevant new messages when notifications are enabled.

Avoid notifying a user about their own messages.

Prevent duplicate notifications where possible.

Allow users to control notification preferences according to the academy's communication policy.

## 11.8 Chat Versus Feedback

Keep the chat and feedback workflows separate.

Chat is for conversation and questions.

Feedback is for structured instructor evaluation and review.

A chat message should not automatically be treated as an official task evaluation.

# PART 12 — FEEDBACK SCREEN: INSTRUCTOR REVIEW AND EVALUATION

## 12.1 Purpose

The Feedback screen provides a structured record of instructor feedback.

It answers:

"What did my instructor review, and what should I improve?"

This is not simply another chat conversation.

Feedback should be attached to an identifiable task, submission, learning activity, or other explicitly defined review item.

## 12.2 Feedback Types

Support:

* Written feedback.
* Voice feedback.
* Task-review status.
* Relevant performance observations.
* Improvement suggestions.
* Review timestamps.

Additional feedback formats may be introduced when justified by the learning workflow.

## 12.3 Feedback Item

A feedback item should include:

* Feedback identifier.
* Academy identifier.
* Student identifier.
* Instructor identifier.
* Related task or submission identifier, where applicable.
* Written feedback.
* Voice-recording reference, if present.
* Review status.
* Creation timestamp.
* Last-updated timestamp.

Do not require a related task for every possible feedback item if the product explicitly supports general student feedback.

## 12.4 Voice Feedback

Instructors should be able to record or upload voice feedback when authorized.

Students should be able to:

* Play.
* Pause.
* Resume.
* Review the recording duration.
* Identify which task or submission the recording relates to.

Voice files must be stored using appropriate storage permissions.

The database should store metadata and the relevant protected resource reference.

The interface must handle unavailable files, playback errors, and loading states.

## 12.5 Instructor Evaluation Workflow

A typical workflow is:

1. An instructor opens a student's assigned work.
2. The instructor reviews the relevant activity or submission.
3. The instructor records the evaluation.
4. The instructor adds written feedback.
5. The instructor optionally attaches voice feedback.
6. The instructor saves the review.
7. The student receives an appropriate notification.
8. The feedback appears in the student's Feedback section.
9. Relevant task status and progress indicators update according to the defined rules.

Do not automatically convert every feedback item into a numerical grade.

Grades should only exist when the academy has defined a grading model.

## 12.6 Pronunciation Feedback

AcademicHub initially supports instructor-provided pronunciation feedback.

For example, an instructor might explain the difference between two similar English sounds or identify a specific pronunciation issue.

Do not claim that AcademicHub automatically evaluates pronunciation unless an actual speech-analysis feature has been implemented.

Automatic speech recognition and pronunciation assessment may be considered as future extensions.

# PART 13 — PROFILE AND SETTINGS

## 13.1 Purpose

The Profile screen manages the student's personal account and application preferences.

Display relevant information such as:

* Profile image or initials.
* Student identifier, if applicable.
* Academy name.
* Assigned course.
* Group name.
* Account information.

Allow the student to update permitted fields.

Potential editable fields include:

* Display name.
* Profile picture.
* Preferred reminder time.
* Theme preference.
* Notification preferences.

* Light mode.
* Dark mode.

## 13.5 Notification Settings

Allow the student to configure:

* Task reminders.
* Upcoming activity reminders.
* Chat notifications.
* Instructor feedback notifications.
* Relevant academy announcements.

## 13.6 Account Actions

Include:

* Edit profile.
* Change password, where supported.
* Sign out.
* Privacy information.
* Terms of service.
* Account deletion or deletion-request workflow, where required and supported.

Do not merely delete a local authentication session while leaving the user believing that their account has been deleted.

## 14.1 Purpose

The web dashboard is the main administrative interface for managing an academy's AcademicHub environment.

It should be designed for desktop productivity while remaining reasonably responsive on smaller screens.

The dashboard must connect to the same underlying backend used by the mobile application.

Changes made by authorized administrators should be reflected in the appropriate mobile views.

Do not implement the dashboard as a static HTML page containing hardcoded sample data.

## 14.2 Dashboard Navigation

The proposed navigation structure includes:

1. Overview.
2. Students.
3. Instructors.
4. Courses.
5. Groups.
6. Schedule.
7. Tasks.
8. Materials.
9. Feedback and Reviews.
10. Announcements.
11. Chat Administration, if permitted.
12. Notifications.
13. Academy Branding.
14. Access Codes and Invitations.
15. Reports.
16. Settings.

Some sections may be combined in the MVP to reduce complexity.

The final navigation should reflect actual implemented functionality.

## 14.3 Overview

Display a concise summary of academy activity.

Possible metrics:

* Total active students.
* Total instructors.
* Number of active groups.
* Upcoming lectures.
* Tasks assigned.
* Task completion statistics.
* Recent activity.
* Pending administrative actions.

Metrics must be calculated from actual database records.

Clearly distinguish between:

* Total accounts.
* Active accounts.
* Enrolled students.
* Students with recent activity.

Do not display fake statistics.

## 14.4 Student Management

Administrators should be able to:

* View students.

* Filter students.

* Assign students to courses.
* Assign students to groups.
* Review relevant learning activity.
* Update permitted account information.
* Generate or assign student identifiers.
* Issue or revoke invitations when authorized.
* Manage account status when supported.

Use pagination or efficient query strategies for larger datasets.

Do not retrieve every student record into the browser and filter everything locally when the dataset could become large.

## 14.5 Instructor Management

Administrators should be able to:

* View instructors.
* Create or invite instructor accounts.
* Assign instructors to groups.
* View instructor-group relationships.
* Manage relevant permissions.
* Disable or revoke access when authorized.

## 14.6 Course Management

Administrators should be able to:

* Create courses.
* Edit course information.
* Configure course categories.
* Assign instructors where relevant.
* Assign student groups.
* Publish or archive courses.
* Associate materials and tasks with courses.

Course structures must be configurable rather than hardcoded to one English curriculum.

## 14.7 Group Management

Administrators should be able to:

* Create groups.
* Assign instructors.
* Assign students.
* View group membership.
* Configure group schedules.
* Manage group-specific resources.
* Configure group communication permissions.

The data model must support multiple groups and prevent inconsistent assignments.

## 14.8 Schedule Management

Administrators should be able to:

* Create lectures or scheduled activities.
* Configure dates and times.
* Assign a group.
* Assign an instructor.
* Modify schedules.
* Record cancellations or rescheduling.
* Notify relevant users when appropriate.

The initial product may assume that each group follows a recurring schedule, but individual schedule changes must also be supported.

Store timestamps consistently and display them in the appropriate local timezone.

## 14.9 Task Management

Administrators and authorized instructors should be able to:

* Create tasks.
* Edit tasks.
* Assign tasks to groups or individual students.
* Configure due dates.
* Add descriptions.
* Configure categories.
* Attach or reference resources.
* Add external practice links.
* Publish tasks.
* Archive tasks.
* Review task completion.

## 14.10 Materials Management

Administrators should be able to:

* Upload resources.
* Add external links.
* Edit resource metadata.
* Assign materials to courses or groups.
* Publish or archive materials.
* Control resource visibility.

* Appropriate type and size validation.
* Upload progress.
* Error handling.
* Storage permission checks.
* Useful success feedback.

## 14.11 Feedback Management

Authorized instructors and administrators should be able to review relevant feedback records.

## 14.12 Academy Branding

* Academy name.
* Academy logo.
* Primary color.
* Secondary color.
* Accent color.
* Optional welcome message.
* Contact details.

## 14.13 Data Import

The academy may already have student information.

The dashboard should eventually support importing records from CSV files.

A safe import workflow should:

1. Accept a supported file format.
2. Validate required columns.
3. Preview the imported data.
4. Detect duplicates.
5. Identify invalid records.
6. Show validation errors.
7. Require confirmation before writing records.
8. Generate an import summary.

Do not create duplicate student accounts silently.

Do not import passwords from ordinary CSV files.

Account credentials and invitations must be handled through secure authentication workflows.

# PART 15 — NOTIFICATION SYSTEM

## 15.1 Notification Categories

AcademicHub should support relevant notifications for:

* New task assignments.
* Upcoming task deadlines.
* Configured daily reminders.
* Schedule changes.
* New instructor feedback.
* Relevant group messages.
* Important academy announcements.

Not every event needs a push notification.

Use notification preferences and sensible defaults.

## 15.2 Notification Channels

The system may use:

* In-app notifications.
* Push notifications.
* Optional email notifications.

Push notifications may use Firebase Cloud Messaging if the application is configured for it.

The backend should determine who receives each notification and whether the event warrants a notification.

The notification system should handle:

* Permission denial.
* Invalid device tokens.
* Duplicate events.
* Notification retries where appropriate.
* User preference changes.
* Removed or expired devices.
* Timezone differences.

Do not rely exclusively on a Flutter timer running in the background to deliver daily reminders reliably.

## 15.4 Notification Content

Notifications should be concise and actionable.

Example:

"Your listening practice is due today."

Another example:

"Your instructor has added feedback to your latest assignment."

Do not include sensitive student information in lock-screen notifications unnecessarily.

# PART 19 — SECURITY AND AUTHORIZATION

Security must be implemented as part of the system architecture rather than added at the end.

## 19.1 Authentication Is Not Authorization

Being logged in does not mean that a user is authorized to access every resource.

Every sensitive operation must verify the user's permissions.

## 19.2 Role-Based Access

Implement appropriate authorization for:

* Students.
* Instructors.
* Academy administrators.
* Future platform administrators.

Permissions should also consider academy membership and resource ownership.

## 19.3 Tenant Isolation

Every academy-specific operation must enforce the correct academy context.

A malicious user must not be able to change an academy ID or document ID and access another academy's data.

## 19.4 Database Security Rules

If Firestore is used:

* Require authentication where appropriate.
* Validate academy membership.
* Validate resource ownership.
* Restrict updates to permitted fields.
* Restrict role changes.
* Prevent unauthorized message creation.
* Prevent unauthorized task completion changes.
* Restrict administrative operations.
* Test cross-tenant access attempts.

Do not rely on Flutter widgets to protect data.

## 19.5 Trusted Backend Operations

Use trusted backend execution for operations that require privileged access.

Examples may include:

* Creating privileged accounts.
* Managing invitation redemption.
* Sending targeted notifications.
* Importing large batches of records.
* Performing administrative operations.
* Enforcing sensitive role changes.

Never embed service-account credentials or private server secrets in the Flutter application or public web bundle.

## 19.6 File Security

Validate uploads and control who can access each resource.

Avoid unrestricted public access to private student feedback or private educational materials.

Do not assume that hiding a download button protects the underlying file.

## 19.7 Privacy

Collect only the data needed for the product's operation.

Provide appropriate privacy information.

Define how user data is retained, exported, and deleted.

Avoid exposing student performance information to unauthorized group members.

# PART 20 — USER FLOWS

Implement and test the following core journeys.

## Flow A: New Student Registration

1. Open AcademicHub.
2. View onboarding screens.
3. Navigate to account creation.
4. Enter name, email, password, and academy invitation code.
5. Validate input.
6. Submit registration.
7. Authenticate the user.
8. Validate academy eligibility.
9. Create or link academy membership.
10. Load the student's academy configuration.
11. Load assigned courses and groups.

## Flow B: Returning Student Login

1. Open the application.
2. Restore the authentication session when valid.
3. Otherwise, show Sign In.
4. Authenticate the student.
5. Resolve academy membership.
6. Load authorized application configuration.
7. Open Home.

Handle expired sessions and revoked memberships correctly.

## Flow C: Student Completes a Daily Task

1. Open Home.
2. Navigate to Plan.
3. Select a date.
4. Open a pending task.
5. Read instructions.
6. Open the external resource if applicable.
7. Complete the task.
8. Return to AcademicHub.
9. Mark the task complete when permitted.
10. Save the completion record.
11. Refresh progress indicators.
12. Display a success confirmation.

## Flow D: Student Opens Educational Materials

1. Open Materials.
2. Browse categories or search.
3. Select a resource.
4. Verify access permissions.
5. Preview, download, or open the resource.
6. Handle errors gracefully.

## Flow E: Student Participates in a Group Discussion

1. Open Chat.
2. Select an authorized conversation.
3. Load recent messages.
4. Send a message.
5. Save the message through the backend.
6. Display the confirmed message.
7. Deliver the message to authorized conversation members.

2. Open an assigned group.
3. Select a student.

6. Add written feedback.
7. Optionally attach voice feedback.
8. Save the review.
9. Update the review status.

## Flow G: Administrator Creates a Task

1. Sign in to the web dashboard.
2. Open Task Management.
3. Create a task.
4. Enter the title and instructions.
5. Select a course, group, or individual student.
6. Configure the due date.
7. Add a resource or external URL when applicable.
8. Preview the task.
9. Publish the task.
10. Create the appropriate student task records or assignment relationships.
11. Trigger notifications when appropriate.
12. Verify that the task appears in the intended students' Plan screens.

## Flow H: Administrator Changes Academy Branding

1. Open Academy Branding.
2. Update the academy name or logo.
3. Select appropriate colors.
4. Preview the configuration.
5. Save the changes.
6. Validate the saved configuration.
7. Make the updated branding available to authorized academy members.
8. Refresh relevant mobile application configuration.

* Changing academy branding.
* Invalid form submissions.
* Duplicate imports.
* Unauthorized administrative operations.

## 22.5 Security Tests

Test explicitly that:

* A student cannot access another student's private feedback.
* A student cannot change their role to administrator.
* An instructor cannot modify academy-wide settings without permission.
* A user cannot read another academy's private records.
* A non-member cannot access a private group chat.
* Unauthorized users cannot access private file resources.
* Client-provided identifiers cannot bypass authorization.

## 22.6 UI Tests

Test:

* Small screens.
* Large screens.
* Light mode.
* Dark mode.
* Empty states.
* Loading states.
* Long text.
* Network failures.
* Keyboard behavior.
* Navigation and back actions.
* RTL behavior if Arabic is introduced.
* Accessibility labels.

Do not treat the absence of visible errors as proof that the entire system is secure.

# PART 26 — DEVELOPMENT WORKING RULES

Follow these rules throughout implementation.

## Rule 1: Understand Before Changing

Inspect the existing project, dependencies, architecture, and designs before making significant changes.

## Rule 2: Preserve Working Functionality

Do not rewrite working components without a clear reason.

## Rule 3: Build Connected Features

A task card, chat screen, or feedback panel must connect to the relevant application logic and data.

## Rule 4: Avoid Fake Functionality

Do not use hardcoded success messages when the backend operation failed.

Do not simulate real-time chat with static messages.

Do not invent student statistics.

Do not create fake upload progress that does not correspond to an actual upload.

## Rule 5: Maintain Consistency

Use shared design tokens, reusable components, consistent naming, and clear navigation.

## Rule 6: Protect Existing Data

Database changes must account for existing records and relationships.

Do not delete existing data merely to make a new schema easier to implement.

## Rule 7: Explain Important Decisions

When choosing a database structure, authentication mechanism, state-management approach, or backend workflow, explain the reasoning briefly.

## Rule 8: Do Not Overengineer

Choose the simplest architecture that satisfies the real requirements.

## Rule 9: Test Incrementally

Test each meaningful workflow before moving to the next phase.

## Rule 10: Be Transparent

Clearly distinguish:

* Implemented functionality.
* Partially implemented functionality.
* Planned functionality.
* External configuration still required.
* Known limitations.

# FINAL PRODUCT DEFINITION

AcademicHub is a customizable, multi-academy learning platform that connects students, instructors, and academy administrators through a unified digital experience.

Its core value lies in bringing together:

* Learning plans and task management.
* Educational materials.
* Group communication.
* Instructor feedback.
* Student progress tracking.
* Academy administration.
* Dynamic academy branding.

The mobile application provides students with a clear and organized learning experience.

The instructor workflows support task review, feedback, and group management.

The web dashboard allows authorized academy personnel to manage the learning environment without modifying the application's source code.

The backend ensures that the information shown to each user is accurate, authorized, and associated with the correct academy.

The long-term objective is to create a reusable software product that can be configured for different language academies while maintaining a consistent, secure, and professional technical foundation.

**Build AcademicHub as a coherent product with working workflows, not merely as a visually attractive collection of screens.**
